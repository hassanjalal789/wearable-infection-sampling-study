# Research overview

*Hassan Jalal · one-page overview, written on 25 September 2026 · new writing, drafted with AI assistance ([contribution statement](CONTRIBUTIONS.md))*

This project examined whether the timing of sparse wearable heart-rate sampling affected retrospective infection warning under modelled energy constraints. The primary analysis did not support an advantage for fixed-clock nighttime sampling over evenly spaced sampling.

**Methods in brief.** I reanalysed two public Stanford COVID-19 wearable datasets ([Mishra et al., 2020](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41551-020-00640-6); [Alavi et al., 2022](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41591-021-01593-2)). The recorded heart-rate and step data of 38 participants with a symptom-onset date were replayed as if a device had sampled on different schedules, each held to the same modelled energy budget: about 5% of continuous sampling, which the model converts into seven ten-minute bursts a day. The primary comparison placed the bursts in a fixed 00:00–06:59 window (S3) or spaced them evenly from midnight (S2). One detector, calibrated per participant to at most two alert-days per person-month, looked for an alert in the 21 days before symptom onset. Schedules were compared participant by participant: a win for earlier or sole warning, a loss for the reverse, a tie otherwise. The [methods guide](METHODS_GUIDE.md) traces each rule to the amendment that set it, and the [method traceability review](METHOD_TRACEABILITY.md) checks each rule against the code.

**Result and uncertainty.** Among 30 participants with an evaluable pair, nighttime sampling won 8, lost 12 and tied 10: θ = 0.433, 95% BCa interval 0.300–0.583, exact two-sided p = 0.5034. θ = 0.5 means no difference; θ is a paired comparison statistic, not an accuracy. H1 was not rejected, so the fixed testing sequence closed and the other comparisons are descriptive only. The interval is wide: before any outcome was seen, the design had 80% power only for large joint effects. See [results and limitations](RESULTS_AND_LIMITATIONS.md).

**Main limitations.**

- A small retrospective sample, and energy that is modelled, not measured; no battery saving is shown.
- The nighttime window is a clock window, not each person's sleep, and 3 of the 7 evenly spaced bursts also fall at night.
- Continuous sampling alerted no more often than the sparse schedules, and no negative-control analysis was run, so how far alerts reflect infection is unresolved.
- Several protocol files remain marked draft, the sensitivity analyses were specified after the result was known, and the method review found planned rules and reporting items that the executed code did not carry out; the [traceability review](METHOD_TRACEABILITY.md) lists them.
